The Hawaii House Question

Can a ten-bedroom creator house in Hawaii pay for itself? Modelled across two islands, buy against lease, four revenue engines, and every tax line the State actually charges. It can — but not as a house, and not on Oʻahu.

The answer

Rent from influencers cannot carry the building. Every configuration needs between 104% and 134% occupancy. Covering costs on rent alone takes $107,000–$136,000 per creator per year — and creators who can pay that do not need a shared house.

One configuration clears a 12% hurdle. Hawaiʻi Island, purchased, with 30-day service terms, location days priced against the film credit and ten brand takeovers a year: 12.93% ten-year IRR at 2.78× debt coverage. That is the 82nd percentile of 600 correlated paths, not the expected case — the median path loses money.

It is not an influencer house. It is a branded-content production venue that keeps a resident roster as inventory.

The sentence that kills the obvious plan

Hawaii pays a refundable film production credit of 22% on Oʻahu and 27% on the neighbor islands, plus five points for hiring locally. Lodging for cast and crew, location fees and airfare to Hawaii are all qualified costs. Then the definitions section arrives:

"Commercial" … does not include an advertising message with Internet-only distribution.

"Qualified production" … does not include … productions produced primarily for industrial, corporate, institutional, or other private purposes. Hawaiʻi Revised Statutes §235-17(o)

A social brand campaign is statutorily excluded. So the play inverts: don't be the taxpayer, be the qualified expense. When a music video or streaming series rents the house, they claim the credit on your invoice — which lets a neighbor island charge 14.71% more than Oʻahu for an identical cost to the customer.

Questions this answers

Can you run a short-term rental influencer house on Oʻahu?

No. Ordinance 22-7 set a 90-day minimum; a federal court permanently enjoined it in December 2023, so enforcement runs on the older 30-day standard. Nothing shorter is available outside resort zoning. Maui's Ordinance 5909 goes further, phasing out roughly 6,200 apartment-district vacation rentals between 2029 and 2031.

Does the Hawaii film tax credit cover social media content?

No. HRS §235-17(o) excludes advertising with Internet-only distribution, and excludes productions made primarily for private or corporate purposes. Music videos, short films, streaming series and TV-distributed commercials do qualify — so the house earns from the credit as a vendor to those productions, never as the claimant.

What is the most profitable use of a bed in a creator house?

Brand activations, at $936 net per bed-night. Cohort retreats and production location days both return $563. Creator residencies return $214 — the least productive use of the asset, and the one that occupies most of the calendar.

How much capital does it actually take?

$2,017,029 — the peak cumulative cash deficit, reached in month 21. That is 45% more than the $1,390,000 down payment plus build-out, because nine months of pre-opening burn and the operating trough both come before any recovery.

How the numbers are computed

Every load-bearing figure is exact rational arithmetic produced by agent-calc — amortization schedules, NPV, IRR and all breakeven solves — composed in Python Fraction, so no floating point enters the chain. No number was produced by a language model. The browser sandbox is a port validated against the exact model to 1.9 × 10⁻⁸ relative, which is float64 rounding in the discounting chain and amounts to fractions of a cent on a million-dollar NPV.

All of it — model, report and sandbox — is open source at github.com/copyleftdev/hawaii-influencer-house.

Revenue assumptions are judgement, not data: there is no public comparable for a Hawaii creator-production house, so the brand-activation pipeline is the load-bearing guess and is treated as such throughout. Tax treatment is directional and depends on facts a Hawaii CPA must confirm. This is a model, not advice — financial, legal or tax.
